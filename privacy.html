2. What we collect

Only what you give us when you get in touch. We do not buy lists, and we do not build a profile of you from other sources.

From the contact form on this site

  • Your name.
  • Your email address.
  • Your message: the description of the task or process you want help with.
  • A hidden timestamp and a hidden empty field, both used only to catch automated spam. Neither says anything about you.

From WhatsApp

  • The messages you send us and the ones we send back, including anything you attach.
  • The phone number you message us from, and the display name your WhatsApp profile shows us.
  • If we speak on a WhatsApp call: the fact that the call happened, with whom and roughly when. We do not record calls. If we ever need to, we will ask you first and you can say no.
  • Notes we type up afterwards about what you said your process looks like.

From email

  • Your email address and name as your mail client sends them.
  • The content of the messages and any attachments.

While we are doing work for you

  • The work contact details of the people whose steps we are mapping, so we know who does what.
  • Examples of the documents a process produces, which may contain personal information about your staff or your customers. We ask for these to be redacted or made up wherever a real example is not necessary, and the contact form says the same thing: keep it general, leave out passwords and customer records.

What we do not collect

  • No analytics, no advertising identifiers, no behavioural tracking. See cookies.
  • We do not ask for ID numbers, bank details, health information, or anything else POPIA treats as special personal information. If a process you want automated touches that kind of data, we will agree in writing what we may see before we see it.

3. Why we collect it

  • To answer you. We cannot reply to an enquiry without a way to reply.
  • To work out whether we can help. Understanding the process you described is the whole first conversation.
  • To quote and to do the work, and to keep a record of what was agreed.
  • To keep our own records where the law requires a business to keep them.
  • To keep the site usable, which here means stopping form spam.

We do not use what you tell us for anything else. We do not use your enquiry as a case study, a testimonial or an example without asking you first, in writing, for that specific use.

4. Our legal basis, in plain words

POPIA says we need a reason before we may process personal information. Ours are:

You asked us to
You chose to fill in the form, send the WhatsApp message or write the email. That is your consent to us using those details to reply. You can withdraw it at any time and we will stop, although that also ends the conversation.
To get a contract going, or to perform one
Quoting for work, agreeing scope and then doing the work all need the details of the process and the people in it.
Because the law tells us to
Tax and company records have to be kept for set periods, whatever we or you would prefer.
Our own legitimate interests, kept narrow
Keeping a record of what was said so there is no dispute later, and blocking spam. We rely on this only where it would not surprise you and does not override your privacy.

Where we rely on your consent, nothing bad happens if you refuse other than our not being able to help.

5. Who else sees it

We do not sell personal information, and we never have. We do not rent it, trade it, or hand it to advertisers or data brokers.

Inside the business, only the two of us see your enquiry. Outside it, your information passes through the services we use to run a business:

WhatsApp (Meta)
If you message us on WhatsApp, WhatsApp carries that conversation. For the content of the messages you send us, we treat WhatsApp as an operator: it processes those messages so that we can receive them, on our behalf. Meta separately decides, as a responsible party in its own right, what it does with the account and usage data WhatsApp generates, under its own terms and privacy policy, which we do not control and cannot change. Message content on WhatsApp is end-to-end encrypted in transit, which means Meta cannot read it, but the fact that you messaged us is visible to Meta. Meta's infrastructure sits outside South Africa, so this is a cross-border transfer; POPIA allows it where the recipient is bound to comparable protection, and we rely on WhatsApp's own terms for that. If you would rather not involve WhatsApp at all, use the contact form or email instead.

7. How we protect it

POPIA requires reasonable technical and organisational measures. In general terms, ours are:

  • Accounts that hold enquiries are protected by strong, unique passwords in a password manager, with multi-factor authentication switched on wherever the service offers it.
  • Connections to this site and to the services we use are encrypted in transit.
  • Access is limited to the two of us, and only to what each of us needs.
  • We keep the number of copies small and delete working copies when a piece of work ends.
  • We ask for redacted or invented examples rather than real customer records whenever a real one is not necessary, so that sensitive data is not in our hands to begin with.
  • If a breach puts your information at real risk, we will tell you and the Information Regulator as soon as we reasonably can, as POPIA section 22 requires.

We describe these in general terms on purpose. No security claim here is a guarantee: no system is perfectly secure, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.

8. Your rights

These are yours under POPIA, free of charge in most cases, and you do not need a reason to ask:

Access
Ask whether we hold personal information about you and ask for a copy of it, with a description of who has had access to it. We may charge the prescribed fee for a copy, and we will tell you the amount before we do any work.
Correction
Ask us to fix anything inaccurate, misleading, out of date or incomplete.
Deletion
Ask us to delete or destroy information we no longer have a reason to hold, or that we obtained unlawfully. Where the law makes us keep a record, we will say so and tell you which law.
Objection
Object, on reasonable grounds, to our processing where we rely on our own legitimate interests. We stop unless we can show a stronger reason to continue.
Withdraw consent
Withdraw consent you gave us, at any time.
Complain
To us, or straight to the regulator. See how to complain.

9. How to complain

Tell us first if you can: it is usually quicker, and we would rather fix it than argue about it.

You do not have to, though. You can complain directly to the Information Regulator (South Africa), the body that enforces POPIA. It takes complaints about how an organisation has handled personal information, and it can investigate and order an organisation to change what it does.

The Regulator publishes its current postal address, email addresses and complaint forms on its own website, at inforegulator.org.za.

We have deliberately not reproduced the Regulator's contact details or complaint-form addresses here, because they change and a wrong address in a privacy notice sends a complaint nowhere. 10. Cookies and tracking

This site sets no cookies and loads nothing from anybody else.

There is no analytics, no tag manager, no advertising pixel, no embedded video, no social widget, no font loaded from a content delivery network and no chat bubble. Every file the page needs is served from bpsra.co.za itself. Nothing on the page reports your visit to a third party, so there is no cookie banner to click away. That is a design decision, and we would like to keep it.

Your browser stores nothing from us. The page works with JavaScript switched off, and the animation on the home page stops by itself if your system asks for reduced motion.
